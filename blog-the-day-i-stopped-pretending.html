Resilience

The Day I Stopped Pretending I Was Fine

← Back to Blog
Resilience 8 April 2026 4 min read

There is a version of resilience that gets talked about a lot. The kind where you push through, keep going, stay strong. Where you smile at the right moments and say "I am doing well, thank you" even when you are not.

I used to think that was what resilience looked like.

I was wrong.

The real turning point — for me, and for many of the people I work with — was not the moment I found strength. It was the moment I stopped performing it.

After going through my own difficult chapter, I noticed something strange. The harder I tried to appear okay, the more exhausted I became. Not from the challenge itself, but from the effort of pretending it was not happening. From the gap between what I felt inside and what I showed the world.

Resilience, I've learned, is not a mask. It's not gritting your teeth and carrying on regardless. It's something much quieter — and honestly, much harder.

It's being able to say: this is hard, and I am still here.

It's allowing yourself to feel the weight of something without being crushed by it. It's knowing that asking for help is not weakness — it is one of the most courageous things a person can do.

When clients come to me, they are often carrying this invisible pressure to already be coping. To be further along in their recovery than they are. To have figured it out by now. And the first thing we do, together, is gently set that expectation down.

You do not have to be fine. You just have to be honest — with yourself, first.

From that honesty, something real can grow. Not a performance of strength, but the genuine article. The kind that holds up not because it is rigid, but because it knows how to bend.

If you are reading this and you are in the middle of something hard — I want you to know that where you are right now is valid. You do not need to be further along. You do not need to have it together.

You just need one honest conversation.

That's usually where everything starts.

Marcelo Marques is a resilience and remission coach working with professionals across the UK and internationally.
